title: MOVE summary: Copy a value into one or more fields, converting it to fit each one. keywords: TO CORRESPONDING CORR copy assign assignment padding truncation justify edited de-editing figurative SPACES ZEROS ---

Syntax

Format 1: copy a value
MOVE { identifier-1 | literal-1 } TO identifier-2 ...

Format 2: copy matching fields of two groups
MOVE { CORRESPONDING | CORR } group-1 TO group-2 ...

What it does

MOVE copies the value on the left into every field after TO. The field it copies from (the sending field) doesn't change. Each receiving field gets the value in its own format: COBOL pads, cuts or converts the value so that it fits the receiving field's PICTURE. Most of what there is to learn about MOVE is how that fitting works.

Moves into alphanumeric fields

When the receiving field is alphanumeric (PIC X) the characters are copied from the left. If the value is shorter than the field, the rest is filled with spaces. If it is longer, the characters that don't fit on the right are lost. A field described with JUSTIFIED RIGHT works the other way round: the value is placed at the right end, spaces fill the left, and extra characters are cut from the left.

Moves into numeric fields

When the receiving field is numeric (PIC 9) COBOL moves the value, not the characters. The decimal points are lined up first. Then:

  • missing digits are filled with zeros, on both sides of the decimal point;
  • digits that don't fit on the left (the high-order digits) are lost, with no warning: moving 1234 to PIC 9(3) leaves 234;
  • extra decimal places are cut off, not rounded (use COMPUTE ... ROUNDED if you want rounding);
  • if the receiving field has no S in its picture, the sign is dropped and the value is stored as positive.

Group moves

If either field is a group item, the move is a plain copy of bytes, treated like an alphanumeric move: no numbers are converted, no decimal points lined up. The bytes are copied from the left, and spaces fill a longer receiving field. This is quick and is often used to copy a whole record, but it puts the sending field's bytes into each item of the receiving group exactly as they are.

Moves into edited fields

A numeric-edited field (one whose PICTURE has editing symbols such as Z, ,, ., $, - or /) is for showing a number to a person. Moving a number into it lines up the decimal point and then formats the digits: leading zeros become spaces or a floating $, commas and the sign are inserted. See PICTURE for the symbols.

The reverse move also works: moving a numeric-edited field to a numeric field reads the number back out of the formatted text (this is called de-editing). The commas, the currency sign and the spaces are ignored, and the sign is kept.

Figurative constants

MOVE SPACES, MOVE ZEROS, MOVE HIGH-VALUES and the others fill the whole receiving field. MOVE ALL "*-" TO X repeats the text until the field is full. See figurative constants.

MOVE CORRESPONDING

MOVE CORRESPONDING A TO B looks for items in group A and group B that have the same name, and moves each one to its partner as a normal elementary move, with the usual conversion. The items don't need to be in the same order or the same format, which makes it a quick way to copy an input record into a print line. FILLER items and items whose names have no partner are left alone.

Examples

"COBOL" is padded with five spaces in a 10-character field and cut to "PROG" in a 4-character one; the JUSTIFIED RIGHT field puts the spaces in front. The numeric moves show zero filling (042), the lost high digit (1234 becomes 234), decimal alignment (7.5 becomes 007.50) and lost low digits (12.3456 becomes 012.34, cut, not rounded). Moving -8.25 to an unsigned whole-number field drops both the sign and the decimals. The last line shows figurative constants, including ALL "*-" repeated to fill six characters.

-1234.5 is formatted with a floating dollar sign, a comma and a trailing minus, and the de-editing move reads the same value back. Moving a plain number into PIC 99/99/9999 inserts the slashes. MOVE CORRESPONDING moves ITEM, QTY and CUST-NO by name, each converted to its new picture, while the FILLER slashes stay. The two group moves copy bytes only: QTY's digits appear as they are stored, and moving X's into ORDER-IN even fills its numeric items with X's, which is why group moves need care.

The signed number -42 moved into a text field arrives as the characters 0042: all four digits, no sign. Going the other way, the text "123" becomes the number 123, and you can then do arithmetic with it.
